# P1.15 — Club-defined roles and permissions, club types

## Context
Read first: `CLAUDE.md`, ADR 0006 (authorization: permissions, policies,
the relationship set), ADR 0018 (club managers, handover, recovery),
ADR 0019 (groups, owners), ADR 0022 (protected identity), ADR 0023 (access
in the database), ADR 0029 (the API only grows), ADR 0039 (notifications),
ADR 0040 (responsiveness), ADR 0042 (history views), `docs/domain-model.md`
("`club_role_assignments`", "`group_memberships`", "`group_owners`",
"Permissions (implemented policies)"), `docs/performance.md`.

**What exists already:**
- **Permissions** are a fixed catalogue in code
  (`packages/shared/src/permissions.ts`, about 80 names). Every endpoint
  declares one; each has exactly one policy in
  `apps/api/src/modules/authorization/authorization.policy.ts` (deny by
  default; the API refuses to start otherwise).
- **Roles are hard-coded and policies name them.**
  - Club roles: `CLUB_ROLES` (chair, treasurer, registrar, club_manager, …)
    in `club_role_assignments`.
  - Group functions: `GROUP_FUNCTIONS` (member, head_coach,
    assistant_coach, team_manager, physio, other_leader) in
    `group_memberships.function`, with a CHECK constraint.
  - Policies ask `actor.hasClubRole(clubId, "club_manager", "registrar")`,
    `actor.hasGroupFunction(groupId, "head_coach", …)`, `LEADER_FUNCTIONS`,
    `BOARD_ROLES` (about 30 places).
  - Role names also appear in SQL (migrations 0001, 0006, 0010, 0021, 0027:
    the last-manager guard, imports, data rights, billing) and in the
    worker (`notifications/recipients.ts`: `gm.function IN ('head_coach', …)`,
    mail and member tasks).
- **Group owners** (`group_owners`, ADR 0019) are an administrative
  relationship separate from functions.
- **The relationship set** (one query, cached in Valkey under a version
  counter that the worker bumps on domain events) holds the person's roles
  and functions; policies run on it per request.
- **No realtime channel** to clients yet (the WebSocket gateway is planned
  for chat, Phase 2). The web app is server-rendered per request; the mobile
  app caches what it was shown (cache first, ADR 0040).
- Seed data and the load scenario assign roles and functions by name.

**Moved here from P1.14** (finding 6 there): invitations to several groups,
each with a role. They are built on this prompt's roles (part A, step 6).

## Goal
A club decides which roles it has and what each role may do, as in AWS IAM
or Google Workspace. ClubAdmin defines the **permissions** (what can be
done); a club administrator creates **roles**, gives them permissions and
assigns them to people, in the club or in a group. A new club starts with a
ready set of roles from its **club type** (sports club, chess club,
pensioners' association, …), which operators maintain, so creating a club
stays as simple as today. Everything is explained in plain language, so that
an administrator without IT experience understands who may do what. Taking a
permission away takes effect at once.

The prompt has two parts, each its own commits and checks:
- **A:** permissions, roles per club, assignment, the role editor with
  help, immediate effect, migration of today's roles. One built-in club type
  (today's roles) is used for new clubs.
- **B:** club types and their role templates, maintained by operators;
  choosing a type when creating a club.

## Concepts (for the ADR and the help texts)
- **Permission (behörighet):** something one may do, defined by ClubAdmin,
  e.g. "Se medlemsregistret", "Planera gruppens aktiviteter". A club cannot
  invent permissions, only combine them. Each permission has a **scope**:
  - *club*: applies in the whole club (e.g. the member register);
  - *group*: applies in the groups where the role is held, including their
    child groups (e.g. planning a group's activities).
- **Role (roll):** a named set of permissions in one club, e.g. "Kassör" or
  "Huvudtränare". A role is either a **club role** (held in the club) or a
  **group role** (held in a group). Club roles may contain group permissions;
  they then apply in every group of the club.
- **Assignment (tilldelning):** a person holds a role from a date, until a
  date or further notice, in the club or in a group. Today's
  `club_role_assignments` and `group_memberships` become assignments.
- **Template (mall):** a club type's set of roles and permissions, copied
  into a club when it is created. Later changes to the template never change
  existing clubs (decision 6).

## Part A — Permissions, roles, assignment

### 1. The permission catalogue
- Each permission in `PERMISSIONS` is classified in code
  (`packages/shared/src/permission-catalogue.ts`):
  - `assignable`: may be put in a role, with its scope (`club` | `group`),
    its **area** (Medlemmar, Grupper, Aktiviteter, Evenemang, Ekonomi,
    Integritet, Föreningen) and whether it is **sensitive** (gives access to
    many people's personal data, protected identity, money, history, or to
    roles themselves).
  - `built_in`: never in a role, decided by relationships alone (one's own
    profile and consents, a guardian's rights for a ward, a member's own
    answers, public pages, operators, billing operations, club creation).
- **Participation** is also expressed as permissions in group scope:
  "Deltar i gruppens aktiviteter" (gets the group's activities in My
  schedule and answer requests; today `member`) and "Räknas som ledare"
  (shown as a leader in attendance and on activities; today
  `LEADER_FUNCTIONS`). This replaces the fixed functions.
- Each assignable permission has catalogue texts (sv, en, nb): a name, one
  sentence in plain language of what it allows, and an example ("Kan till
  exempel lägga till en träning eller ställa in en match"). A test fails for
  an assignable permission without texts.
- MFA follows the permission, not the role: sensitive permissions keep the
  policies' `mfa` requirement as today.

### 2. Policies ask for permissions, not role names
- `Actor` gains `holds(permission, scope)`: club scope (`clubId`) or group
  scope (a `GroupRef`, matching the group or an ancestor). Policies replace
  `hasClubRole(...)`/`hasGroupFunction(...)` with `holds(...)`, and keep
  their relational conditions (archived group, `onBehalfOf`, the resource's
  club, MFA, protected identity). One policy per permission stays (ADR 0006).
- A lint rule or test refuses role or function names in policies and in
  the worker's recipient queries (except the locked roles, step 3).
- **Group owners** stay a relationship (ADR 0019) and keep their rights
  (decision 4); policies keep `ownsGroup(...)` alongside `holds(...)`.
- SQL: a function `app.holds_permission(p_club uuid, p_permission text,
  p_group uuid DEFAULT NULL)` (STABLE, `SECURITY INVOKER`, through the
  `app.*` helper pattern of ADR 0023) replaces role names in RLS helpers and
  triggers, except the locked manager role. The worker's recipients use
  `holds`-based SQL ("who holds `group.waitlist.manage` in this group").

### 3. Roles per club, locked roles and safeguards
- **Club manager (Föreningsansvarig) administers access; it does not hold
  everything** (maintainer's decision 2026-11-08). The locked part of the
  role is one built-in permission, **`club.roles.manage`
  ("Administrera roller och behörigheter")**: create, change and delete
  roles and assign any role to anyone, including permissions the manager
  does not hold. The manager does *not* thereby have a treasurer's or a
  registrar's permissions: what the role may do besides administering
  access is an ordinary, editable set of permissions. The built-in template
  gives it the club's administration (club settings, groups and owners,
  invitations and join requests, handover of managers, the subscription
  and plan, the roles' and the audit log's read access) and nothing of the
  day-to-day work of other roles (the member register's details, money
  beyond the subscription, attendance, consents); the ADR lists the split
  per permission. To find whom to assign, role administration includes a
  name list of the club's members (through the person presenter), nothing
  else about them.
- **Self-assignment** is allowed, so that a small club where one person
  does everything is never stuck and a club can never lock itself out: a
  manager may give themselves any role. It is shown, audited
  (`role.assigned`, `self: true`) and told at once to the club's other
  managers and the chair (notice, mandatory email); the roles page lists
  "Roller som föreningsansvariga gett sig själva".
- The rest of ADR 0018 stays: the last-manager rule, handover and recovery;
  the role cannot be deleted and its `club.roles.manage` cannot be removed,
  so the SQL that names `club_manager` stays valid.
- **Chair (Ordförande)** stays locked for recovery
  (`club.managers.claim`), its other permissions editable (decision 3).
- **No escalation for everyone else:** a person without
  `club.roles.manage` (e.g. a group owner assigning group roles) may assign
  a role, or invite with it, only if every permission in it is one they
  hold themselves. Club managers are exempt through `club.roles.manage`,
  not through holding everything. The same rule applies to invitations
  with roles (step 6).
- Deleting a role that people hold needs a replacement role or ends the
  assignments (the dialog says which and how many); history keeps them.
- A role keeps a stable `template_key` (e.g. `head_coach`) while it comes
  from a template, for the API's old enum fields and for new permissions in
  later releases (decision 5).

### 4. Immediate effect when permissions change
- **Server side, at once:** changing a role's permissions, or an
  assignment, bumps the club's authorization version (one Valkey counter per
  club, read in the same MGET as the relationship set). The next request
  of every holder is decided on the new permissions; there is nothing to
  log out of, since the API authorizes every request. The in-transaction
  bump is also published as a domain event for the worker.
- **Jobs:** background jobs started by a person (exports, imports) check
  the permission again when they run and stop with a notice if it is gone.
- **Clients:** every API response carries `x-authz-version` (the club's
  and the person's version). The web app re-reads the menu and page on the
  next navigation anyway; a form posted without the permission gets a
  friendly page ("Dina behörigheter har ändrats") instead of an error. The
  mobile app compares the header with its cached value and refreshes its
  permissions and drops cached screens it may no longer see; a silent push
  (`authz.changed`, IDs only) to the holders' devices makes this happen
  without a tap. When the WebSocket gateway arrives (Phase 2), it carries
  the same event and closes subscriptions the person may no longer hold.
- **People are told:** holders of a role get a notice when a permission is
  removed from it or their assignment ends (category `club_admin`, in-app),
  naming the role and the permission in plain language.

### 5. The role editor (web; pedagogical first)
- **Roles page** (club settings → "Roller och behörigheter"): club roles
  and group roles as cards, each with its name, a one-line summary in plain
  language generated from its permissions ("Kan se medlemsregistret och
  planera gruppens aktiviteter"), how many hold it, and badges for
  sensitive permissions.
- **Editing a role:** permissions grouped by area, each with its name,
  sentence and example, a "?" that opens the longer help; sensitive ones
  marked with an explanation of why. "Kopiera roll" starts from an existing
  role; "Återställ till mallen" restores a template role.
- **Before saving:** a summary of the change in sentences, and its effect:
  "3 personer får inte längre se medlemsregistret: Anna L, …" (names
  through the person presenter; protected people as "en medlem").
- **Who may do what:** per permission, the list of roles and the number of
  people who hold it ("Vem kan …?"); per person (their profile), every role
  and what it allows. "Visa som" (viewing as, existing) lets an
  administrator check a role's view.
- **Help:** a short page "Så fungerar roller" with a diagram (permission →
  role → person, club or group), linked from every role screen; first-visit
  hints on the roles page; texts reviewed for plain language (no
  "permission", "scope" or "policy" in the UI: "behörighet", "gäller i",
  "roll").
- Undo is through history (ADR 0042: the role's history panel shows each
  change and who made it).
- Mobile: read-only (one's own roles and what they allow); editing is web
  only.

### 6. Assignment and invitations (from P1.14 finding 6)
- Club roles are assigned on the person's page as today; group roles in
  the group's member list, where the function choice becomes a choice of
  the club's group roles.
- **Invitations to several groups, each with a role:** the invitation form
  shows a matrix of the club's active groups (rows, filterable by name and
  kind, the inviter's own groups first) with a choice of group role per
  ticked row, default the club's participant role. Club roles can be preset
  as today (the role preset becomes a club role ID; MFA as today).
  - Data: `invitation_groups (club_id, invitation_id, group_id, role_id)`
    (RLS, history, unique per invitation and group, at most 50 rows); the old
    `invitations.group_id` and `rolePreset` keep working (ADR 0029).
  - Accepting adds every row in one transaction (archived groups skipped,
    "already in group" ignored, the existing audit events).
  - Allowed: `club.invitations.manage` as today, and per row the no-
    escalation rule (step 3) plus `group.members.manage` in that group for a
    role other than the participant role (`group_role_not_allowed`).
    Group owners may invite to their own groups (decision 7).
  - The invitation list and the acceptance page show groups and roles.
- One role per group per invitation; several roles in one group are
  assigned afterwards (decision 8).

### 7. Data and migration (expand/contract, forward-only)
- New tables, all with `club_id`, RLS (`app.enable_tenant_rls`), history
  (`history.enable_history`), `created_at`/`updated_at`:
  - `roles (id, club_id, scope club|group, template_key NULL, locked,
    name_sv, name_en, name_nb NULL, description per language NULL,
    sort_order, archived_at)`; display name falls back to the catalogue
    text while a template role is not renamed (decision 9);
  - `role_permissions (club_id, role_id, permission)`, unique, index on
    `(club_id, permission)` for "who holds";
  - `invitation_groups` (step 6).
- Columns: `club_role_assignments.role_id`, `group_memberships.role_id`
  (nullable first, backfilled, then `NOT NULL` in a later migration);
  `role`/`function` stay, filled from the role's `template_key` (or `NULL`
  for custom roles) by a trigger, so old readers keep working.
- The migration creates every existing club's roles from the built-in
  template (today's roles and today's effective permissions, from
  `domain-model.md` "Permissions"), backfills assignments, and is safe on a
  live database (batches per club).
- A test proves that every persona in the demo scenario has the same
  allowed and denied endpoints before and after, **except the club
  managers'** day-to-day permissions that the new template leaves to other
  roles; those differences are listed explicitly in the matrix test (one
  table of intended changes), and the demo managers who also do that work
  (e.g. Anna in SVK) get the matching roles in the seed. Existing clubs
  are migrated with the same split; their managers are told which
  permissions moved to which roles and how to give them back to themselves.
- Audit events (rule 3): `role.created`, `role.updated` (permissions added
  and removed), `role.deleted`, `role.assigned`, `role.unassigned`.
- Person references: assignments are already declared in
  `<module>.references.ts`; check the merge of duplicates covers `role_id`.

### API (part A)
| Method | Path | Permission | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| GET | `/api/v1/permissions` | authenticated | The catalogue with texts in the caller's language; cacheable |
| GET | `/api/v1/clubs/:clubId/roles` | `club.roles.read` (new) | Roles with permissions and holder counts |
| POST | `/api/v1/clubs/:clubId/roles` | `club.roles.manage` | No escalation; MFA |
| PATCH | `/api/v1/clubs/:clubId/roles/:roleId` | `club.roles.manage` | Locked roles refused; `If-Match` on the role's version |
| POST | `/api/v1/clubs/:clubId/roles/:roleId/preview` | `club.roles.manage` | Effect of a change: who gains and loses what (no write) |
| DELETE | `/api/v1/clubs/:clubId/roles/:roleId` | `club.roles.manage` | `replacementRoleId` or end assignments |
| GET | `/api/v1/clubs/:clubId/permissions/:permission/holders` | `club.roles.read` | Keyset-paginated; people through the presenter |
| GET | `/api/v1/me/permissions?clubId=` | authenticated | The caller's permissions in the club (apps' menus), with `authzVersion` |
Existing assignment endpoints gain `roleId` (and keep the enum fields);
invitations gain `groups: [{ groupId, roleId }]`.

### Permissions (part A)
- New `club.roles.read` (club managers, registrars and the chair by
  default), `club.roles.manage` as today (club managers; sensitive, MFA).
- Allow and deny tests for every new policy; matrix rows for every new
  endpoint (including tenant isolation: club B gets 404 for club A's
  roles); a test for the no-escalation rule and for the locked roles.

## Part B — Club types and templates
- Global tables (no `club_id`; RLS through the `app.*` helpers, ADR 0023;
  read by everyone logged in, written by operators): `club_types (id, key,
  names per language, description, active, sort_order)`,
  `club_type_roles (club_type_id, key, scope, names, descriptions, locked)`,
  `club_type_role_permissions`. History on all three.
- The built-in type "Idrottsförening" (today's roles) is created by the
  migration; operators add types (e.g. "Schackklubb", "Pensionärsförening",
  "Frimärksklubb") in the operator console, with their roles and
  permissions, using the same editor as clubs (part A step 5).
- Creating a club (and the club creation request) asks for the type, with
  one sentence and the role list per type; the club gets a copy.
  `clubs.club_type_id` records it (for statistics and later templates).
- A club can compare its roles with its type's template and copy a role
  from it ("Lägg till från mallen"); templates never change clubs by
  themselves (decision 6).
- The country pack may supply default type names and a default type per
  country.

## Decisions (confirmed by the maintainer 2026-11-08)
1. **Name in the UI:** "Behörighet" for permission, "Roll" for role,
   "Föreningstyp" for club type (default).
2. **Club roles and group roles both** become club-defined in this prompt
   (default), not only group roles.
3. **Locked roles:** the club manager role is locked only in
   `club.roles.manage` (administering roles and assigning any role, without
   holding the permissions; self-assignment allowed, shown and told to the
   other managers); its other permissions are editable. The chair is locked
   only for recovery. (Decided 2026-11-08.)
4. **Group owners stay** a relationship next to roles (default).
   Alternative: owner becomes a group role with the group's admin
   permissions.
5. **New permissions in later releases:** each new permission declares the
   template role keys that get it; a migration adds it to the clubs' roles
   with that `template_key`, never to custom roles, and never if sensitive
   (those are offered to the club managers in a notice) (default).
6. **Template changes never reach existing clubs** by themselves (default);
   clubs can copy roles from the template.
7. **Group owners may invite to their own groups** (default yes).
8. **One role per group per invitation** (default).
9. **Role names:** a name per club language with fallback to the
   catalogue text for template roles not renamed (default), so that a
   Norwegian member sees "Hovedtrener" until the club renames the role.

## Users and roles involved
Club managers (edit roles), registrars and the chair (read roles), every
member (sees their own roles and what they allow), group owners (assign
group roles in their groups), operators (club types, part B).

## Privacy and security
- Changing roles changes who sees personal data: sensitive permissions are
  marked, need MFA, and every change is audited and in history; holders are
  told when they lose a permission.
- Club managers administer access without holding every permission;
  self-assignment is visible and told; no escalation for others; the
  locked role prevents lock-out; permissions apply on the
  next request.
- The "who holds" lists and the preview show names only through the person
  presenter (protected identity: "en medlem").

## Protected identity
Holder lists, the change preview and invitation lists show people through
`PersonPresenter`; nothing in roles stores names.

## Locale and country packs
Catalogue texts (name, sentence, example) for every assignable permission
and every template role in sv, en and nb; the help page in all three;
club type names in all three; country packs may set default types.

## Performance
- `holds(...)` is a set lookup on the relationship set plus the club's
  role map (cached per club under its version; the same MGET as today, one
  more key). Request budgets must not grow; add cases for the roles page,
  the holders list (keyset) and `/me/permissions`.
- `app.holds_permission` is indexed by `(club_id, permission)` and
  `(club_id, role_id)`; query plans tested on the load scenario.
- The migration runs per club in batches.

## Seed data
- The demo clubs get the built-in template; SVK renames one role and adds a
  custom group role ("Materialansvarig" with "Planera gruppens
  aktiviteter"), FIL removes a permission from "Lagledare".
- Part B: a second type "Schackklubb" (roles "Ordförande", "Turneringsledare",

- Personas in `seed-data.md` for the role editor and for a person who loses

## Acceptance criteria
- [ ] Policies and the worker ask for permissions, not role names (lint or
      test); the permission matrix is unchanged for the demo personas
- [ ] Club managers create, edit, copy, delete and assign any role without
      holding its permissions; self-assignment is shown and told to the
      other managers; others follow the no-escalation rule; locked roles
      cannot be broken
- [ ] Removing a permission takes effect on the holders' next request; the
      apps refresh their menus; jobs re-check; holders are told
- [ ] The role editor explains every permission in plain language, shows
      the effect before saving and who holds what; the help page exists
- [ ] Invitations to several groups, each with a role (from P1.14)
- [ ] Club types with templates; a new club gets its type's roles (part B)
- [ ] Old API fields (`role`, `function`, `groupId`, `rolePreset`) keep
      working (`pnpm api:breaking` clean)
- [ ] Tenant-isolation and allow/deny tests for every new endpoint and
      policy; audit events and history for every role change
- [ ] Translations complete for sv, en and nb
- [ ] Docs: a new ADR (club-defined roles, amending ADR 0006, 0018, 0019),
      `domain-model.md`, `performance.md`, `seed-data.md`, the README

## Out of scope
- Conditions on permissions beyond club and group scope (e.g. "only
  members under 18", time windows).
- Roles across clubs (federations, districts).
- Changing built-in permissions (own data, guardians, operators).
- The WebSocket gateway itself (Phase 2); this prompt only defines the
  `authz.changed` event it will carry.
