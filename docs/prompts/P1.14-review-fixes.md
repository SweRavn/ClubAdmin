## Context

## Findings

### 1. Recurring trainings: calendar-like rules, open-ended series, an answer request before every session

**What exists (ADR 0034, ADR 0035, P1.7):**
- `activity_series` stores **weekly slots only** (ISO weekday, local start
  time, length; one slot per weekday), a first and a last date (both
  required), skipped dates (`skipped_dates`) and whether the country pack's
  public holidays are skipped. Sessions are **materialised** when the
  series is created; a series covers **at most 400 days**
  (`series_too_long`). There is no horizon job.
- Deleting one session of a series adds its date to the skipped dates
  (later changes do not bring it back); a session with answers or
  attendance is cancelled instead of deleted. Changes apply from a date;
  "this and following" splits the series.
- Responses: `response_mode` (`none`, `optional`, `requested`; trainings
  default to `optional`) and a deadline in minutes before each session,
  copied from the series to its sessions. Members see the session in "My
  schedule" as soon as it exists and may answer at any time before the
  deadline.
- Notifications: `response.reminder` goes out **24 hours before the
  deadline** to those who have not answered (`notifications:response_reminders`,
  every 15 minutes). There is **no notice that asks people to answer** a
  given session, and nothing the leader can time.
- Terminology: in this code base a **call-up** (`callup`, ADR 0036) is the
  squad picked for one activity (usually a match); it narrows who takes
  part. What the maintainer calls "kallelsen" for a recurring training is
  read here as the **answer request** to everyone in the group ("are you
  coming?"), sent a chosen time before each session (see decision 1).

**Wanted:**
1. **Recurrence like Google Calendar.** A leader sets how a training
   repeats with the familiar presets and a custom rule:
   - every week on one or more weekdays (today's behaviour), each with its
     own start time and length (a slot per weekday, as today);
   - every *n* weeks (every other week, every third week …);
   - monthly on a weekday by position: "first Wednesday", "second and
     fourth Tuesday", "last Friday" of the month;
   - monthly on a day of the month: "the 15th" (a month without that day
     skips it, as Google does);
   - (daily, and every *n* days, for camps and intensive weeks).
   The rule is stored as a strict subset of RFC 5545 RRULE (`FREQ` =
   `DAILY`/`WEEKLY`/`MONTHLY`, `INTERVAL`, `BYDAY` with an optional
   ordinal such as `1WE`/`-1FR`, `BYMONTHDAY`, `UNTIL` or none), validated by
   a shared Zod schema, evaluated in the club's time zone by a pure,
   tested function in `packages/shared` (no new dependency unless one on
   the allow list is clearly better), and exported to calendar feeds as
   the sessions they are (feeds keep listing sessions, not rules).
   Existing series become `FREQ=WEEKLY` rules with their slots
   (data migration in the same release, expand/contract).
2. **Open-ended or with an end date, and switchable.** A series has an end
   date or runs "until further notice". Either can be changed later:
   - end date → until further notice: sessions are added ahead as below;
   - until further notice → end date (or an earlier/later end date):
     sessions after the new end are removed, except those with answers or
     attendance, which are cancelled (ADR 0035), exactly as other rule
     changes do; a later end date adds the missing sessions.
   Changes apply from a date (today by default, never in the past), as
   today; past sessions are never rewritten.
3. **Removing single sessions** (school holidays, a hall that is closed):
   "Delete this session" stays as it is (the date becomes a skipped date).
   Add removing **a range of dates** at once ("no training 2026-12-21 –
   2027-01-06": every session of the series in the range, same rules) and
   show the removed dates on the series with a way to bring one back
   (restoring a date re-creates the session from the current rule unless
   the session still exists cancelled, which is then restored).
4. **An answer request before each session, at a chosen time.** On the
   series (and on a single activity) the leader chooses whether and when
   the group is asked to answer: "N days/hours before the start" (e.g. 3
   days before, at the start time of day, or a fixed local time such as
   18:00 three days before). A job sends each session's request once, at
   that moment, to everyone who takes part (the roster, ADR 0036: with a
   call-up only those called up) and, for children, to the guardians who
   may answer for them, through the notifications catalogue (new type,
   e.g. `response.requested`, category `responses`, push and inbox; email
   per the person's settings). Members who already answered get nothing.
   The existing reminder before the deadline stays. Sessions do not appear
   any later in the schedule because of this: the request is a notice, not
   a visibility rule (see decision 2).

**Data model (expected):**
- `activity_series`: `rule` (jsonb, the parsed RRULE subset with the slots'
  times and lengths), `ends_on` nullable (null = until further notice),
  `materialized_until` (date), `request_lead` (e.g. minutes before start,
  or a days-before plus local time; nullable = no request). The old
  `slots`/`last_on` columns are kept and backfilled until a later contract
  step (rule 13).
- `activities`: `request_lead` copied like the deadline, and `requested_at`
  (when the request went out; idempotence) with a partial index on the
  send time of unsent requests.
- Skipped dates stay on the series (`skipped_dates`), now also written by
  the range removal.

**Materialising open-ended series (ADR 0034 amendment, decision 3):**
sessions are still rows (attendance, answers and the schedule read model
depend on that). An open-ended series is materialised a fixed horizon
ahead (e.g. 13 months) and a daily job extends every open-ended series to
the horizon (club by club, one transaction each, idempotent by
`occurrence_on`), within the request budgets. A series with an end date is
materialised to its end as today; the 400-day limit applies only to how
far ahead sessions exist, not to how long a series runs.

**API (expected; ADR 0029: only additions):**
- `POST /groups/:groupId/activity-series` and the series' `PATCH` accept the
  `rule`, `endsOn` (nullable) and `requestLead`; the weekly-slot fields
  remain accepted and are translated to a weekly rule.
- `POST /activity-series/:seriesId/skipped-dates` (a date or a range) and
  `DELETE /activity-series/:seriesId/skipped-dates/:date` (bring one back).
- The series and its sessions return the rule, its human-readable summary
  is produced by the clients from the rule (sv, en, nb).
- Request budgets for the new endpoints; the extension job and the request
  job get query-plan tests on the load scenario.

**UI:**
- Web, series form: a "Repeats" control like Google Calendar (does not
  repeat, every week, every other week, monthly on the first <weekday>,
  monthly on day N, custom …) with a custom dialog (every *n*
  days/weeks/months; weekdays; monthly by day or by position; ends never /
  on a date), the times per weekday, the answer request ("Ask the group to
  answer: N days before at HH:MM / no request"), and a plain-language
  summary ("Every other Wednesday at 18:00, until further notice").
- Series page: the rule's summary, the removed dates with "bring back",
  "Remove dates…" (a date or a range), and changing the end (date or until
  further notice).
- Mobile: shows the summary on the session and the new notice; editing
  series stays on the web.

**Tests:** the rule evaluator per case (positions, last weekday, months
without the 31st, intervals across the year end, DST changes in the club's
zone, holidays skipped); changing between open-ended and an end date in
both directions, with and without answers on the removed sessions; range
removal and restoring; the request job (sent once, at the right moment in
the club's zone, only to those who take part and have not answered,
guardians for children, nothing for cancelled sessions or when the request
is off); permission matrix rows and tenant isolation for the new
endpoints; the extension job is idempotent.

**Decisions (defaults, to confirm):**
1. **"Kallelse" for trainings = the answer request to the group** (not the
   call-up of ADR 0036, which stays for picking a squad). Default: yes.
   Alternative: a timed call-up that narrows the roster per session.
2. **When a session becomes visible.** Default: as today, as soon as it
   exists (members see the whole season and may answer early); the
   request only notifies. Alternative: hide each session until its request
   goes out.
3. **Open-ended series.** Default: materialise 13 months ahead and extend
   daily (the ADR 0034 rule "no horizon, no nightly job" is amended for
   open-ended series only). Alternative: keep the 400-day limit and require
   an end date, offering "extend" when it nears.
4. **Request timing options.** Default: "N days before, at HH:MM local
   time" (N 0–14) and "N hours before the start" (1–72). Default value for
   new training series: no request (as today); the club may set its own
   default later.
5. **Rule subset.** Default: DAILY, WEEKLY, MONTHLY with INTERVAL, BYDAY
   (with ordinals 1–4 and −1), BYMONTHDAY; no YEARLY, no BYSETPOS beyond
   the ordinals, no COUNT ("after N sessions") — say if COUNT is wanted.
6. **Holidays.** Default: "skip public holidays" stays per series and also
   applies to the new rules.

### 4. Valkey connection errors in the web app during development

**Found:** after some time `pnpm dev` showed `[ioredis] Unhandled error
event: write EPIPE / ECONNRESET` and then `MaxRetriesPerRequestError` on a
page. The web app's Valkey client had no error listener and no TCP
keep-alive: an idle connection dropped by Docker's port forwarding or WSL2
(e.g. after the computer slept) failed the next command, and two retries
were not enough while it reconnected.

**Fixed already (commit `fix(web): keep the Valkey connection alive …`):** the
web client keeps the connection alive (TCP keep-alive), reconnects at once
on a reset and resends the command, waits up to five retries, and logs one
warning per outage instead of unhandled error events; the API's and the
worker's clients get the same keep-alive. Verified by killing the
connection with `CLIENT KILL` and reading again.

**Root cause found later:** the errors persisted with the new client:
Valkey was healthy (`valkey-cli ping` inside the container gave `PONG`, one
client connected, no rejected connections), but every connection from the
host to `127.0.0.1:6379` was reset. Docker's port forwarding to the
ten-day-old container had broken (WSL2 sleep or network change), while
`pnpm dev:doctor` reported the service healthy because it only asked Docker.
`docker restart clubadmin-valkey-1` restores the forwarding. Fixed: the
doctor now also probes Valkey and PostgreSQL through their published ports
from the host (PING, SSL request) and says to restart the container when the
port does not reach it.

**Still to do:** the login page and other pages show a readable message
("the session store cannot be reached; run `pnpm dev:doctor`") instead of
Next.js's error overlay when Valkey cannot be reached.

### 5. Members cannot ask to join a group with direct admission

**Found:** a member of SVK opened the group "Herr senior" and found no way to
apply or queue. The group's admission mode is `direct` (the demo data), and
a member may apply only to `waitlist` groups, or to `direct` groups when they
are full (ADR 0043, `domain-model.md` "Applying"); otherwise only owners and
club managers add members. The group page shows no button and no
explanation, so the member has to ask a leader outside the system.

**Wanted:**
- Members (and guardians with `act_on_behalf` for a child) may apply to a
  `direct` group also when it has free places: "Ansök om plats" creates a
  waiting-list entry as today (same table, same position shown to the
  applicant), and the owner admits it with one click (`admit`), or removes
  it with a reason. No offer step is needed when there is a free place, but
  the existing offer flow stays available.
- **The people who can admit are told.** Today `waitlist.entry.created`
  reaches only the applicant (and whoever applied for them). Add a notice
  `waitlist.entry.received` (category `waitlist`, in-app and push; email
  per the recipient's settings) to everyone who may admit: the group's
  owners (`group.waitlist.manage`), or the club managers when the group has
  no owner. It names the group and links to its waiting list; the
  applicant's name only through the person presenter (protected identity:
  "a member" without the name, ADR 0022). For both `direct` and
  `waitlist` groups; a waiting list with many entries is batched by the
  digest, not one push per entry. Recipient tests as for the other types.
- `waitlist` groups keep "Ställ dig i kö" as today; a full `direct` group
  behaves as today (queue).
- `closed` groups show a short explanation on the group page ("Gruppen tar
  inte emot ansökningar; kontakta en ledare") instead of nothing; members
  already in the group see nothing new.
- The button's label follows the situation: "Ansök om plats" (direct, free
  places) or "Ställ dig i kö" (waitlist, or full); the applicant's own entry
  is shown instead of the button once they have applied (position, withdraw).
- Applying from outside the club (club search, `open-groups`) is unchanged:
  only `waitlist` groups with a publicly visible list are listed there.

**Decisions (defaults, to confirm):**
1. Applying to a `direct` group with free places needs the owner's
   admission (default), rather than joining at once without anyone deciding.
2. The group form explains the three modes in one line each, so that club
   managers choose knowingly ("Direkt: medlemmar ansöker och ledaren antar;
   Kö: …; Stängd: …").

**Docs:** amend ADR 0043 (applying to `direct` groups with free places),
`domain-model.md` ("Applying"), the help text; tests for applying to a
`direct` group with and without free places, the `closed` explanation, and
the permission matrix rows if an endpoint changes (none expected: the
existing application endpoint accepts `direct` groups); the notice
`waitlist.entry.received` in the catalogue (sv, en, nb) and in
`docs/notifications.md` if it lists the types.
