## Goal

Anna opens "Subscribe in my calendar" on the web or in the app, chooses
"My schedule, with Elsa and Oskar", and her phone's calendar shows every
training and match of both children in both clubs, titled "Elsa: Training
F2017", updated when a coach moves or cancels a session. In the event's
description are two links, "Elsa is coming" and "Elsa is not coming";
tapping one answers for Elsa without logging in, with the same deadline
and roster rules as the app. Erik, who coaches F2017, subscribes to the
group's calendar. If a phone is lost or a link is forwarded, the owner
revokes the feed and the URL and its answer links stop working at once.
Calendars never show who else is coming, and a protected member never
appears in anyone else's calendar.

## Users and roles involved

Anyone with a login and a schedule (members, guardians with
`view_schedule`, leaders) creates "My schedule" feeds; answer links appear
for the people they may answer for (themselves, wards with `sign_up` or
full guardianship). Readers of a group's calendar (`group.activities.read`)
create group feeds. Holders of `club.activities.read` (club managers,
registrars, board roles) create club feeds. Nobody else; a calendar app
fetching a feed acts with the feed owner's current rights, read-only.

## User stories

- As a parent, I want one subscription with my own and my children's
  activities across clubs, so that the family calendar is complete.
- As a parent, I want to choose which children a feed includes, so that I
  can give my ex-partner's shared calendar only the child we share. (See
  open decision 5.)
- As a parent or teen, I want to answer "coming" or "not coming" from the
  event in my phone's calendar with one tap, so that I need not open the
  app.
- As a coach, I want to subscribe to my group's calendar on my phone.
- As a board member, I want the club's calendar (all groups) in my
  calendar.
- As a subscriber, I want cancelled sessions to show as cancelled and moved
  ones to move, so that my calendar is never wrong for long.
- As a subscriber, I want to see my feeds, when each was last fetched, and
  revoke or regenerate one, so that a lost phone or forwarded link is no
  risk.
- As a guardian whose `view_schedule` right was removed, I expect the
  child's activities to disappear from my feed at the next refresh.

## Data model

- **`calendar_feeds`** (global table, person-owned; RLS through the
  `app.*` access helpers, ADR 0023: the owner reads and writes their own
  feeds; the feed lookup below is the only other path): `id`,
  `person_id` (owner), `scope` (`my_schedule`, `group`, `club`; the enum
  may grow, ADR 0029; open decision 1 on `club_public`), `club_id` and
  `group_id` (null for `my_schedule`; `group` needs both, `club` the club;
  composite foreign key `(club_id, group_id)` to `groups`), `person_ids`
  (`uuid[]`, `my_schedule` only: whose activities, the owner and chosen
  wards; empty means "me and every ward I may see", open decision 5),
  `label` (optional, 60 characters, e.g. "Family calendar"), `token_hash`
  (sha256 of the secret, unique), `last_fetched_at` (written at most once
  an hour per feed), `revoked_at`, `revoked_reason` (`owner`,
  `regenerated`, `access_lost`, `unused`, `erased`; a code),
  `created_at`, `updated_at`. Indexes: unique `token_hash`,
  `(person_id) WHERE revoked_at IS NULL`. At most 10 open feeds per
  person (`too_many_feeds`). Record history with `token_hash` redacted and
  `last_fetched_at` ignored
  (`history.enable_history('public.calendar_feeds', redact => '{token_hash}',
ignore => '{last_fetched_at}')`), every column classified in the history
  view policy; `app.enable_updated_at`. Declare `person_id`
  and `person_ids` in `calendar.references.ts` (merging duplicates keeps
  feeds of the kept person; a feed of the merged person is revoked).
  Update `domain-model.md` (the columns replace `scope_id` and
  `include_children`).
- **The secret:** 32 random bytes, base64url, shown once when created or
  regenerated; the URL is `webcal://<host>/cal/<secret>.ics` (and an
  `https://` form for Google). Regenerating revokes the old feed
  (`regenerated`) and creates a new one with the same settings.
- **Feed lookup:** `app.find_calendar_feed(p_token_hash)` (SECURITY
  INVOKER, `app.lookup = 'calendar_feed'` while its one query runs, a
  `lookup_read` policy on `calendar_feeds`; ADR 0032 pattern) returns the
  open feed's `id`, `person_id`, `scope`, `club_id`, `group_id`,
  `person_ids`, and whether the owner's account is active. Revoked and
  unknown tokens look the same.
- **`schedule_entries.sequence`** (integer, expand with default 0, filled
  from `activities.sequence` in the migration and written with the rest of
  the row): the feed needs `SEQUENCE` without joining `activities`. Also
  `meet_place` and the location's address if the feed shows them (open
  decision 3); otherwise no change.
- **Answer links are not stored.** A link carries `feedId`, `activityId`,
  `personId`, `status` and `HMAC-SHA256(CALENDAR_TOKEN_KEY,
"calendar-answer:" + those four)`, base64url. It is valid while the feed
  is open, the activity has not ended (ADR 0021: bounded by the activity)
  and the feed owner may still answer for the person. Rotating the key
  invalidates all links (feeds keep working; the next fetch carries new
  links). `CALENDAR_TOKEN_KEY` in `.env.example` with a dummy value; the
  API refuses to start with feeds enabled and no key.
- **Retention:** revoked feeds are deleted 30 days after revocation; feeds
  not fetched for 12 months are revoked (`unused`) and their owners told
  (a catalogue notice, open decision 10); `privacy.erase_person` deletes the
  person's feeds (a new migration replaces the function). Worker job
  `calendar:retention` daily (one statement each; the table is global, so
  no loop over clubs). Add the rule `calendar_feeds` to the retention
  registry and its row to the table in `docs/gdpr.md`.

Write ADR 0044, "Calendar feeds: lookup, acting as the owner, ETags and
answer links", recording: the global table and its
lookup; that a fetch acts as the owner with their **current** rights,
read-only, and returns an empty calendar when they have none left; one
VEVENT per activity (no `RRULE`: sessions are materialised; the series
mapping waits for iMIP in Phase 2); UTC times; content-hash ETags; the
answer-link format and the confirmation step; and what an event contains.
Amend ADR 0021 with a pointer.

## Feed content (iCalendar, RFC 5545)

- `VCALENDAR` with `PRODID:-//ClubAdmin//<version>//EN`, `VERSION:2.0`,
  `CALSCALE:GREGORIAN`, `METHOD:PUBLISH`, `X-WR-CALNAME` (translated:
  "Strandvikens VK – F2017", "My schedule"), `X-WR-TIMEZONE` (the owner's
  club or locale default), `REFRESH-INTERVAL;VALUE=DURATION:PT1H` and
  `X-PUBLISHED-TTL:PT1H`.
- One `VEVENT` per activity in the range **60 days back to 400 days
  ahead** (`CALENDAR_MAX_DAYS`), at most 3,000 events (the oldest past
  ones dropped first): `UID:<activityId>@<APP_DOMAIN>` (stable across
  feeds and later iMIP), `DTSTAMP`, `DTSTART`/`DTEND` in UTC (`Z`), or
  `VALUE=DATE` for all-day activities, `SEQUENCE` (from
  `schedule_entries.sequence`), `LAST-MODIFIED` (`changed_at`),
  `STATUS:CONFIRMED` or `CANCELLED`, `SUMMARY`, `LOCATION`, optionally
  `GEO`, `DESCRIPTION`, `URL` (the activity page), `CATEGORIES` (the kind).
- `SUMMARY`: the title, prefixed for `my_schedule` feeds with the names of
  the owner's wards the activity is for ("Elsa: Training", "Elsa, Oskar:
  Joint training"; not for the owner's own activities), through the
  presenter with the owner as viewer; cancelled ones prefixed with the
  translated "Cancelled:".
- `DESCRIPTION` (plain text, translated, in the owner's language): group
  names; meeting time and place; the cancellation reason; for each person
  the owner may answer for and who is on the roster (call-ups applied):
  their current answer ("Elsa: coming") and, while answers are open, the
  two links; the deadline ("Answer by Thursday 18:00", club time zone);
  a footer "Subscribed via ClubAdmin – manage at <url>". **Never** notes,
  leader notes, other people's names, counts or attendance.
- What is in a feed:
  - `my_schedule`: exactly the rows `GET /me/schedule` returns for those
    people (the same service call: memberships on the day, wards with
    `view_schedule`, activities with a call-up only for those called up and
    for leaders), from clubs where `calendar_feeds` is granted.
  - `group`: the group calendar (`groupCalendar`), while the owner holds
    `group.activities.read`; activities with a call-up appear with the
    note "only those called up" (as on the group page). Answer links only
    for the owner's own family members on the roster (open decision 4).
  - `club`: the club calendar, while the owner holds
    `club.activities.read`; no answer links.
- **Activities of events (P1.6)** appear like any other activity (they are
  in `schedule_entries`); the event's registration is not part of the feed.
- Serialisation: a small in-house serialiser in `packages/shared` (or the
  `calendar` module) with escaping (`\\`, `;`, `,`, newlines), line folding
  at 75 octets on UTF-8 boundaries, CRLF, and unit tests against RFC 5545
  examples; or an allow-listed library (open decision 2). Validate the
  output with a parser in tests (dev dependency only, allow list).

## API

Feed management under `/api/v1` (login, own data; writes need an active
club for `group` and `club` feeds, ADR 0031). Problem codes, never values:
`too_many_feeds`, `feed_scope_invalid`, `calendar_feeds_unavailable`
(entitlement), `answer_link_invalid`, `answer_link_expired`, plus the
response codes of P1.5 (`response_closed`, `not_on_roster`,
`activity_cancelled`).

The web app serves `/answer/[token]` (public, no login): it shows what will
be answered ("Elsa is coming to Training F2017, Tuesday 17:30") and one
button that posts (CSRF-protected server action through the BFF, which
calls the API without a user token); after it, "Saved" with a link to "My
schedule". `GET` never changes anything, so link scanners and calendar
previews cannot answer (open decision 7). Answer links point to
`PUBLIC_WEB_URL/answer/<token>`.

**Request budgets (ADR 0040):** cases for `GET /me/calendar-feeds`, the
create endpoints, `GET /cal/{secret}.ics` (a guardian of two children in two
clubs; the group feed of F2017) and the answer `GET`/`POST`; their cost must
not grow with the data. A fetch runs at most one transaction at a time (the
lookup, then the schedule as the owner).

**Acting as the owner:** the feed and answer handlers load
`relationshipSet(feed.person_id)` and build the owner's actor (never their
roles beyond what the schedule paths read), then call the same service
methods with a `withActor`/`withTenant` context of the owner, marked in
audit and history as `via: calendar_feed` (answers are on behalf of the
ward, ADR 0006). They re-check on every use: the account is active, the
ward is still in `schedulePeople` (feeds) or `respondablePeople`
(answers), the feed is open. Feeds write nothing except `last_fetched_at`
(hourly, outside the request's critical path).

## Permissions

- Group and club feeds reuse `group.activities.read` and
  `club.activities.read` for creation (matrix rows for the new endpoints:
  allowed for the group's members, guardians with `view_schedule`, leaders,
  owners, managers; denied for others in the club; 404 for club B).
- `/me/calendar-feeds*` and `/cal/*`, `/calendar-answers/*` need no
  permission: own data or a secret, like `/me/schedule` and invitation
  lookups. List them in `domain-model.md` ("Endpoints for my data"; public
  endpoints with secrets).
- No new permission; if the implementation needs one (e.g. for the club
  feed), add it with one policy, allow/deny tests and matrix rows.
- Tests: a relative with only `view_schedule` gets no answer links; a
  revoked `view_schedule` removes the ward at the next fetch; a member not
  called up does not see the match; a leader does; another club's group
  feed cannot be created (404).

## Privacy and security

- A feed URL is a bearer secret that reveals where and when children are
  (ADR 0021, `security.md`): stored as a hash, shown once, never logged
  (redact `/cal/*` and `/calendar-answers/*` paths and the `token` in
  request logs and traces; tests), revocable and regenerable, `Referrer-Policy:
no-referrer` on the answer page, no third-party resources on it.
- Rate limits (Valkey, `security.md` table): `/cal/*` 60 per minute per
  feed and 300 per minute per IP (unknown tokens count double against the
  IP); `/calendar-answers/*` 20 per minute per IP and 10 per minute per
  token. `429` with `Retry-After` (calendar apps retry later).
- Calendar data contains only title, times, place, group names, status,
  the subscriber's family's own answers and answer links (ADR 0021): no
  attendee lists, counts, notes, other people's names; the names in
  `SUMMARY` are the owner's own wards.
- Answers through links are audited like app answers
  (`activity.response.set` with `via: "calendar_link"`, on behalf of the
  ward) and publish `activity.response.changed`; notes cannot be given
  through links.
- Audit events: `calendar_feed.created`, `.updated`, `.regenerated`,
  `.revoked` (with the reason; system for `access_lost` and `unused`),
  registered in `packages/shared` `audit-actions.ts` and rendered in the
  audit log (P1.9: an own sentence or a verb; keep the verb list short).
- Notices (ADR 0039): `calendar.feed.revoked` to the owner when the system
  revokes a feed (`unused`, `access_lost`), with the reason code and no
  secret; recipients tested.
  Feed fetches are not audited (only `last_fetched_at`).
- Legal basis: the membership (running the activity) at the subscriber's
  request; the feed exists only while the owner keeps it. Record in
  `domain-model.md` and `docs/gdpr.md` ("download my data" lists feeds
  without secrets, P1.8).

## Protected identity

- Feeds name people only in `SUMMARY` and the answer lines, and only the
  owner's own family members as the presenter shows them to the owner. No
  feed ever lists other participants (ADR 0021, ADR 0022).
- A protected ward in a guardian's feed: the leaked-URL risk is higher for
  them (their place and times). The create dialog warns when a chosen
  person is protected and names them by initials in the feed (open
  decision 8).
- Group and club feeds contain no people at all.
- The answer page shows only the first name of the person answered for,
  through the presenter with the feed owner as viewer.

## Performance

- `/cal/*.ics` p95 < 50 ms (`performance.md`): one lookup, one cached
  relationship set, the "My schedule" statement over 460 days (one
  statement, the index `schedule_entries (group_id, starts_at)`; measure
  for a guardian of two children in two clubs on the load scenario),
  serialisation in memory. Cache the rendered body per feed in Valkey for
  60 s keyed by feed ID and a version that bumps on
  `activity.changed`/`activity.response.changed`/membership and
  guardianship changes (or simply 60 s: open decision 9), so frequent
  polls of the same feed cost one Valkey read. `304` when the ETag
  matches.
- A group feed over a season (≈ 200 events) < 50 ms; a club feed of a
  large club (40 groups, ≈ 8,000 events, capped at 3,000) < 150 ms
  (documented exception, measured).
- Answer POST < 200 ms (as `PUT …/responses/:personId`).
- Load scenario: feeds for 5 % of guardians; a timed test in
  `schedule-plans.integration.test.ts` (or a new `calendar-plans` test)
  and a query-plan test (no sequential scans).

## Out of scope

- Email invitations with Accept/Decline (iMIP, `METHOD:REQUEST/REPLY`,

- A public club calendar without a login (needs public activities and
  public club pages, Phase 3).

## Decisions (2026-11-07, all as recommended)

1. **Club scope:** ADR 0021 says "the club's public calendar", but no
   activity is public and public pages are Phase 3. Recommended: implement
   `club` (the club calendar for `club.activities.read` holders) now, keep
   `club_public` reserved for Phase 3, and say so in the ADR 0021
   amendment.
2. **iCalendar library:** an in-house serialiser (about 150 lines, no
   dependency, full control of escaping and folding) or `ical-generator`
   (MIT, on the allow list). Recommended: in-house; a parser such as
   `ical.js` (MPL-2.0, allowed) only as a dev dependency for tests.
3. **Event details:** ADR 0021 lists title, time, place, group, status and
   links. Recommended: also meeting time and place, the location's address
   and the cancellation reason (all shown to everyone in the app, no
   personal data), never notes.
4. **Answer links in group feeds:** none, or for the owner's own family on
   the roster. Recommended: the owner's family only (a coach who is also a
   parent answers from one calendar); none in club feeds.
5. **Choosing people in "My schedule" feeds:** fixed list (`person_ids`)
   or "me and all my wards" that follows new wards automatically.
   Recommended: both; empty `person_ids` means "everyone I may see", so a
   new child appears without a new feed, and a list serves shared custody.
6. **Feed URL path and host:** `/cal/<secret>.ics` on the web host
   (proxied by the Next.js app to the API) or directly on the API host
   (`api.<domain>`). Recommended: unprefixed `/cal/*` on the API, routed by
   Caddy on the main host straight to the API (no BFF hop, better budget),
   so the URL is `webcal://<APP_DOMAIN>/cal/<secret>.ics`.
7. **One click or confirm:** ADR 0021 says "one-click"; a `GET` that
   answers can be triggered by link scanners and previews, which would
   answer both ways. Recommended: a confirmation page with one pre-chosen
   button (one tap after opening the link); amend ADR 0021 accordingly.
8. **Protected wards in feeds:** full first name, initials, or excluded
   unless the guardian confirms. Recommended: initials in `SUMMARY` and on
   the answer page, plus a warning when creating the feed.
9. **Feed caching:** a 60-second Valkey cache per feed (simple; changes
   show within a minute plus the client's refresh) or event-driven
   versions. Recommended: 60 s; calendar clients refresh far less often.
10. **Notice when the system revokes a feed:** a type in the `account`
    category. Recommended: `calendar.feed.revoked` with `mandatoryEmail` and
    `immediate` (a feed the owner relies on stopped working; it also keeps
    `account` an email-only category, see "What exists already"), in-app
    too.

## As implemented (2026-11-07)

Everything above, with these differences and additions:

- **Range:** a feed shows 30 days back and 370 days ahead (inside the
  400-day cap), at most 3,000 events.
- **No `ical.js`:** the feed is checked in tests with a small in-house
  parser (`packages/shared/src/calendar/ical.test.ts`, the API's
  `calendar.integration.test.ts`) instead of a dev dependency. The manual
  import into Apple Calendar, Google Calendar and Thunderbird is not done
  yet (it needs a public URL); it is part of the release check.
- **Answer-link tokens:** base64url of feed, activity and person IDs, the
  answer byte and a 16-byte HMAC (87 characters), because Fastify limits
  path parameters to 100 characters. The web page is `/answer/<token>`.
- **Request budgets:** `GET /me/calendar-feeds` and both feed kinds
  (measured with `CALENDAR_FEED_CACHE_SECONDS=0`, i.e. rendered). The answer
  endpoints read one schedule entry and are left out; `POST
/me/calendar-feeds` from the app is listed as not measured.
- **Merging people** moves the merged person's feeds to the kept person
  but not `person_ids`: a list that names the merged duplicate keeps its old
  ID until the owner changes the list (feeds for "everyone" follow at once).
- **Seed:** the demo feeds are written directly as the persona with fixed
  secrets (the service would make random ones).
- **Mobile:** the settings screen creates a feed for me and all my children
  and opens its `webcal://` URL; choosing people, renaming, new links and
  removing are on the web ("Manage on the web"). The server reports
  `calendar.feeds` in `/meta/client` unless `FEATURE_CALENDAR_FEEDS=false`.
- **Caddy:** only the development Caddyfile exists in the repository; it
  routes `/cal/*` on the main host to the API. A self-hosted proxy needs the
  same route (ADR 0044).
- **Notices:** the owner is told only when the system revoked a feed
  (`unused`, `access_lost`), not when they removed or replaced it.
